The [[South Indian coffee filter|SoFi]] is designed to take 15-25g coffee, and according to Aramse, can be brewed [three ways.](https://aramse.coffee/products/sofi#sofi-brews) To date I mostly brew the decoction, its version of espresso - a 6:1 ratio brew that can be drank straight, diluted, or used in lattes.
## Decoction

> [!Recipe]
> - Set up your SoFi with the brew chamber[^1] atop the catch chamber[^2].
> - Put 15g coffee in the brew chamber, ground espresso fine.
> - Pour 30g water over the coffee and bloom for a minute.
> 	- This step needs special care to soak all the grounds evenly and delicately; you should pour on a cupping spoon to break the flow, or use a Melodrip to make it automatic.
> - After the bloom, pour 60g water in, put the cap on, and walk away. Come back in 15 minutes.
> 	- Pouring this step is a little easier but you *should* still use the cupping spoon/Melodrip.

Admittedly the brew time is a little absurd, but I think it's what makes this brew special. You get a super even extraction like long immersion brews, but you still get acidity from the bloom. It ends up tasting like a well-dialed in turbo without being anywhere near as challenging to pull off. You do retain basically all the oils in the cup, which is a matter of taste - I like it, but most people probably won't.

Traditionally you'd use this decoction to make filter kaapi. As a 6:1 ratio brew, I find it pleasant enough to drink straight (although it will be cold).
## Filter
If you want to make filter, I generally make the decoction and then just dilute it with hot water. I find a diluted decoction is where the oils in the cup really shine, as it's such a different experience than a paper-filtered pour over.

Aramse do list a recipe on their site that promises filter coffee in 3 minutes, as well as one for cold brew. I may try them for completion's sake, but generally I've never needed to do so since my [[My dripper collection|(far too many)]] drippers do the task just fine.
## General notes
I want to make a full writeup about this at some point, but I've long suspected that the SoFi is so unique because of atmospheric pressure. I have three chief points of evidence:
- SoFi brews drip slower the longer they go on.
- After the first minute or so, it becomes hard to separate the chambers of the SoFi.
- It is sometimes necessary to loosen the connection between the top and bottom chambers to unclog stalled brews or to lessen brew time on particularly bothersome coffees.
I think during brewing, the brew and catch chambers have a semi-airtight seal with each other, which sets up pressure alterations. How the pressure is altered, I'm not sure. Observations 2 and 3 point to the catch chamber dropping in pressure, but I'd expect that to speed up brews. It wouldn't make sense for pressure to be different in the brew chamber too unless the cap is applied, but as I note later on, the cap's effects are inconsistent.

It's notable that Aramse broke this seal by adding holes into the walls of both chambers in their V2 model. This removes the variable of the seal between the chambers, but it would shorten brew times (probably also a good thing).
### Using the cap
My theory with the cap is that placing it on traps a partial vacuum in the brew chamber due to the steam from the hot water. This would theoretically exert an upward force on the water in the chamber, slowing brew times. In practice, sometimes putting the cap on does slow the brew, sometimes it doesn't. I find the SoFi to brew in its 7-15min range even without the cap.

The reason for the inconsistency here is probably because the cap isn't made with tight enough tolerances around the top chamber. This makes sense; it's just meant to cover the brew chamber, not hold up to weight or resist getting dislodged.
### Using the filter screen
When I bought my SoFi from Aramse, they were very clear that they do not provide a filter screen with their filter. It seems like a strange omission, but by their testing, all it does is risk slicing up your fingers.

After testing with a filter from a grocery store, I find myself agreeing with the general sentiment. The filter screen should theoretically serve two purposes: one, to evenly distribute water over the coffee grounds, and two, to tamp the coffee grounds and contain them. In practice, though, the filter screen mostly diverts water to the edges instead of distributing the water evenly. I've even seen it 'capsize' after too much water flowed to one side of the bed. Plus, there's no real reason to ever tamp the coffee bed.

[^1]: Brew chamber being the chamber of the SoFi with the perforated bottom. This is the top chamber.
[^2]: Catch chamber being the chamber of the SoFi with the solid bottom. This is the bottom chamber.
