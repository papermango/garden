The noble South Indian coffee filter is one of the first coffee brewing methods I've ever used. It's found in most Indian homes, crucial to make filter kaapi, and is the worst named brewing implement I can think of. (How has nobody thought of a better name??)[^1]

[^1]: My particular South Indian coffee filter has a name: the [SoFi](https://aramse.coffee/products/sofi), made by Aramse. I'll steal their name for this article.

While it's probably classified as a vernacular coffee brewer, the South Indian coffee filter offers a lot to a specialty coffee enthusiast - it's a no-bypass brewer capable of both filter and near-espresso, and its lack of paper filtration makes an incredibly silky brew. And for limited spaces & budgets, it's a cheap way to get consistent, high-quality (near) espresso.
## What it is
The SoFi is kind of a mix between a phin and a French press. It's two thin, hollow cylinders, one with perforations in the bottom, a cap, and often times a sort of tamper/filter screen (which mine omitted). Like much of Indian cookware, it's always metal[^2].

[^2]: Usually either steel or brass. I've mostly seen steel, which I'd recommend anyways.

To use it, you stack the cylinders so the one with perforations is on top, put ground coffee in the top cylinder, pour boiling water in, then let gravity do the work. Traditionally, you'd place the filter screen on the coffee and the cap on top to seal everything in. I say *traditionally*, because these two pieces have [[SoFi brewing notes#Using the cap|inconsistent]] [[SoFi brewing notes#Using the filter screen|effects]] on the brew.

For consistency, it's best to use a more premium version of this than what you find at Indian grocery stores. I own a V1 SoFi which lacks the airflow holes on the V2 but otherwise is essentially a uniform, precision-manufactured South Indian coffee filter. This is what I used at the UIUC Coffee Club to prepare espressos.

Unfortunately for folks in the US, the SoFi is pretty expensive since it's exclusively shipped from India by Aramse's stockist Kaveri. I'd still shell out if you're interested in it; I've found no analog for what it can make. The OXO Rapid Brewer may be the closest thing that exists to it.
